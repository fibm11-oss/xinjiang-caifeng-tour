    City walk

    Dry heat and oasis, woven into one land

    The red Flaming Mountains, green Grape Valley, cool karez wells and the vast Jiaohe ruins — extremes side by side.

    Turpan, China's lowest basin (Aydingkol Lake, -154 m) and hottest summer city, is also a millennial Silk Road hub. The red Flaming Mountains, green Grape Valley, cool karez wells and the vast Jiaohe ruins — Turpan's City Walk is full of extremes: dry heat and oasis, desolation and prosperity, intertwined on one land. In Mongolian, "Turpan" means "rich and fertile land"; after a visit you'll see how fitting the name is.

    The route at a glance

    • 09:00–10:30 Old town & Emin Minaret
    • 10:30–12:30 Karez Folk Park
    • 15:00–18:00 Grape Valley (afternoon escape)
    • after 20:00 Night Market (dinner)
    • Extension (by car): Jiaohe Ruins, Flaming Mountains (by season/stamina)

    Turpan's urban core is small — the old town, Emin Minaret and Karez Park are close, walkable or a short taxi; Grape Valley, Jiaohe and the Flaming Mountains need a half-day car. We strictly follow the local iron rule — out early, rest at noon, out again at night — never let you suffer under midday sun.

    Old town & Emin Minaret (09:00–10:30)

    Start in the old town around Gaochang Road, where Uyghur homes show earthen-yellow walls, carved doors and windows, and grape drying rooms — the most vivid Xinjiang everyday. Nearby, the Emin Minaret, an Islamic Qing-era tower linked to the mosque beside it, is Turpan's landmark; yellow brick forms delicate geometric patterns, stunning against blue sky. For Western-Region history, the Turpan Museum (mummies, painted pottery, documents; closed Mondays) explains "why grapes grow here" starting from the karez.

    Karez Folk Park (10:30–12:30)

    The karez, with the Great Wall and the Grand Canal, is one of China's three ancient hydraulic projects. An underground corridor system, it channels Tianshan snowmelt to nourish the oasis; the dark channels stay about 18°C year-round — step in and the heatwave is gone. That is how Turpan people fought drought for millennia. The park shows the full structure of vertical wells, dark and open channels, great for kids' science. At noon, cool off and learn at once.

    Grape Valley (15:00–18:00 afternoon escape)

    A 5A scenic area, ten-thousand-mu grape colonnades lush green, mostly seedless white and mare's-teat varieties. Temperatures run 5–8°C below the city — a natural summer oasis. In August when grapes ripen, step into a Uyghur farmyard for just-picked grapes and folk dance, the most comfortable afternoon. Frog Lane is good for slow wandering; the Wang Luobin Music Art Museum tells of the composer's bond with Xinjiang; the Dawazi folk park has high-wire shows. Grape Valley also hosts "XICHUAN COFFEE" with two shops — the Afanti Manor shop is a red standalone embraced by vineyards, serving "naan coffee" poured into a just-baked naan cup, plus local specials of mulberry, grape and apricot with coffee beans; the Grape Valley乐园 shop sits in the busiest photo spot, past the intangible-heritage走廊 and stage, with a lake pavilion boat to sip coffee and desserts while cruising, cool and relaxed.

    Jiaohe Ruins (dawn or next day, by car)

    The capital of the Cheshi Front Kingdom from the 2nd century BCE, the world's largest and oldest raw-earth architecture city, over 2,000 years old. Built of rammed yellow earth with no walls, its streets, offices, temples and homes' ruins are vast at dawn. Enter by shuttle early to cut sun exposure; summer nights even host the "Jiaohe Night" live concert. The most striking lesson of the Silk Road cities, worth a dedicated trip.

    Flaming Mountains (dawn/dusk, by car)

    The prototype of the Flaming Mountains in Journey to the West, red cliffs majestic; inside the park a giant golden-cudgel thermometer is the icon, with sculptures of the banana-fan tale. Note: at noon ground temperature exceeds 70°C — no long hikes; come at dawn or dusk for an hour. In fact a roadside viewpoint already shows the whole range; the park isn't mandatory.

    Night Market (after 20:00)

    When the sun sets and temperature drops, Turpan's night truly wakes. Around the Grape Night Market and the old downtown, baked buns, pilaf (with raisins and carrot), naan-oven meat, cold noodles, yoghurt rice pudding line up, with iced apricot tea to wash away the heat. Fruit is sold by the kilo; seedless raisins and dried apricots, sweet and juicy, are the most dignified souvenirs.

    Locals know: Turpan is two hours behind the interior, and August days hit 38–42°C with ground over 70°C at noon — so locals "go out 7–10, hide indoors at noon, move again after 19:00". At A-level parks, if you consent to leave contact info when buying tickets, don't miss the repeat-visitor perks; buy dried fruit at the city farmers' market, more choice and authentic; watch the weighing.

    Restaurants & cafés to try

    Turpan is the extreme of "hot" and "sweet" — the midday heat pushes locals' main meal to evening, so dinner and the night market are the highlight. The picks below cluster in the Gaochang old town and Grape Valley; cafés are exotic little shops that appeared only in the last two years, perfect to cool a sunny itinerary. Some old shops close afternoons — check hours before going.

    Where locals eat

    • Kuoximakela Baked Buns: 260 East Old Town Rd, crust shatters at a light tap, even the base crisp; lamb filling tender, no gaminess, cumin-rich — the essence of old Turpan. Best fresh in the morning.
    • Sulaiman Banmian King: 365 East Old Town Rd (opposite museum south gate), the "you-rou-rou" poured sauce sizzles, noodles chewy, every strand coated; also a local pilaf spot.
    • Halike Special Pilaf (Turpan flagship): distinct grains, refillable, chicken-leg fragrant, big lamb chunks — carbs and fat redefined as satisfaction.
    • Sila Special Chilli Chicken: courtyard full of wok-fire, chicken crisp outside tender inside, layered spicy secret sauce — a rare "chilli-chicken specialist" in Turpan.

    Cafés & drinks

    • Qingtangli Coffee: 353 Middle Gaochang Rd, photogenic inside and out, a sip transports you to the Middle East; refined desserts. Light is best around 15:00.
    • Yinuo Coffee Roasters: partnered with the Turpan Library, modern decor with earthen pottery and mural paintings; coffee and a book together, history and life seeping in, quiet and restful.
    • Lizamate Manor Café: in the Murale community of Grape Town, open to the public, a few cats to pet; try coffee with special yoghurt rice pudding for afternoon tea — a surprise. Close to Grape Valley and karez, easy to combine.

    Practical information

    • Getting there: Urumqi high-speed rail reaches Turpan North in 1.5 hrs; city sights by taxi/bus; Jiaohe, Flaming Mountains, Grape Valley by car to save time.
    • Best season: Jun–Oct (July–Aug fullest fruit, Sept–Oct most comfortable); spring mulberries (late Apr–mid May) also worth it.
    • Suggested stay: city + Grape Valley one day; Jiaohe and Flaming Mountains need two; Kumtag Desert in Shanshan, separate.
    • Dress & sun: sunscreen first — SPF50+, wide-brim hat, sun mask, sunglasses, breathable sun shirt; thick non-slip soles against hot ground; 3 L water daily; a light jacket at night.
    • Local notes: Jiaohe and karez have almost no shade, don't push at noon; ask before photographing in Uyghur villages; limited dining in scenic areas, prefer the city.
