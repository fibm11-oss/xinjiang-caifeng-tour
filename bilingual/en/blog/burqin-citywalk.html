    City walk

    A tiny border town worth an overnight

    Fairy-tale Russian architecture, Northern Xinjiang's most famous riverside night market, and China's only river flowing to the Arctic Ocean.

    Burqin, a mini border town embraced by the Irtysh River, is the must-pass staging post to and from Kanas and Hemu. Most travellers treat it as a waypoint — but trust me, Burqin deserves at least one night. Here are fairy-tale Russian buildings, the most famous riverside night market in Northern Xinjiang, and China's only river flowing to the Arctic Ocean. "Burqin" comes from Oirat Mongolian, meaning "the riverside place where three-year camels graze"; locals' Kazakh name "Kuigan" means the confluence of two rivers — the Burqin River joins the Irtysh here. Enter the town and read the river first; that is how you understand Burqin.

    The route at a glance

    • 17:00–18:30 Burqin Bridge + Irtysh River
    • 18:30–20:00 Sino-Russian Old Dock Street
    • 20:00–22:30 Riverside Night Market (dinner + supper)
    • Optional: Wucaitan (Colourful Beach) sunset, ~24 km northwest, ~30 min by car

    Burqin's City Walk is easy: the county town is small, walkable in half a day, but the border-town ease stays with you. We focus on "dusk to late night" — a river sunset, then the night market's smoke and fire.

    Burqin Bridge + Irtysh River (17:00–18:30)

    The Burqin Bridge spans where the Burqin River meets the Irtysh — the opening frame for river and sunset views. The Irtysh is China's only river to the Arctic Ocean; snowmelt gives it a milky emerald. From the bridge, birch trees line the water upstream and downstream, and at dusk pink-orange clouds warm the little town. Many stop here on the way back from Wucaitan, capturing "long river + small town" in one frame.

    Sino-Russian Old Dock Street (18:30–20:00)

    This one-kilometre stretch of colourful Russian-style spired wooden houses along the Irtysh is a regional-level night cultural hub and an irreplaceable landmark on the Northern loop. Once a Sino-Soviet shipping port, the old dock ruins and shipping memorial still hold border-trade memory; today the open block is dotted with wooden walkways, light bands and ethnic sculptures. In peak season (Jun–Sep) vendors appear from 19:00, with 20:00–23:30 the busiest; occasional rawap flash mobs. The long river feeds the town; grilled fish sizzles in the dark as travellers from all sides pause briefly.

    Riverside Night Market (20:00–22:30 dinner + supper)

    The soul of Burqin nights is the riverside night market by the Irtysh. The whole market's signature is cold-water fish — grilled pike (Chuertai) is the TOP1, firm and boneless from cold water, crisp outside and tender inside over red-willow charcoal; plus wudaohei, huachizi wild fish, red-willow kebabs, smoked horse sausage, handmade yoghurt, sea-buckthorn juice. Kvass, bread-honey naturally fermented, cuts the kebab grease.

    Evening bonus: bars along the Qicai River

    After the night market, Burqin's true night soul is along the Qicai River — an artificial waterway linking the Burqin and Irtysh, with swan bridges, lotus towers and glass walkways, plus a water-light show after dark in summer; a whole row of riverside bars and fairy-tale B&Bs lines the bank. Stroll north from the Kanasi Bridge (internet-famous) at dusk as the lights come on and the river mirrors the bridge — the town's most healing night view.

    Side trip: Wucaitan (Colourful Beach, sunset, by car)

    If you have half a day more, the 24-km northwest Wucaitan is worth it — China's only water-side Yadan, the Irtysh flowing through, with valley forest opposite, "one river, two scenes". Arrive 1.5–2 hours before sunset (Beijing time ~21:00–22:00 for the best slant light). The Aktetbek cable bridge crosses to the south bank. Many string "Wucaitan sunset + riverside night market" into one complete Northern Xinjiang dusk.

    Locals know: Burqin is the "fairy-tale border town", its houses painted bright yellow, mint green and pink, like illustrations from a storybook; founded as a county in 1919, 21 ethnic groups — Russian, Kazakh, Mongol, Han — coexist, and Russian bread, kvass and spired wooden houses are all marks of a century of trade. Best travel is Jun–Oct (summer escape, Sept golden autumn brightest); winter brings the Jikepulin ski resort. The county is 122–140 km from Kanas (2.5–3 hrs); most use it as the last supply stop before the mountains — but give the night market at least one evening.

    Restaurants & cafés to try

    Burqin is the "last supply stop" before Kanas, but don't treat it only as a transfer — the night market's grilled pike, Russian dining and the riverside coffee are worth an extra night. Cold-water fish is cooked to order; confirm before you order. Cafés are fewer than in big cities, but a few independents match any.

    Where locals eat

    • Riverside Night Market: grilled pike, wudaohei, lamb skewers, baursak… liveliest after dusk, open to late night in peak season. Deeper alleys are more authentic.
    • Katyusha Russian Restaurant: borscht, Russian levain bread, braised meat in pot — authentic, the taste legacy of a century of Sino-Russian trade. For a quiet Western meal, just right.
    • Awule Restaurant: air-dried-meat pilaf and hand-grabbed lamb are superb, proper nomad home flavours, calmer than the night market, good for settling the stomach.
    • Xiaobaiyang Night Market: friendly, open to 02:00, the place to continue after the main market closes. Skewers, fried noodles, a little wine — perfect for a few friends.

    Cafés & drinks

    • Kekesong Café: Bojin Bay, rated 4.6, every detail thoughtfully arranged, among the town's公认 best independents. A latte with European spired wooden houses outside, slow rhythm instantly.
    • Mr. Bear Coffee: inside the Qicai River project, opens 07:00 and runs to past midnight — catch it before an early bus or after a night out; friendly.
    • Banke Leisure Café: on Shenhu Road, sit as long as you like, 07:00–23:30, a sweet with it and an easy afternoon; the first choice when you want a foothold.

    Practical information

    • Getting there: walkable in town; Wucaitan ~30 min by car; Beitun railway station has coaches to the county.
    • Best season: Jun–Oct (summer escape, Sept brightest); winter skiing at Jikepulin.
    • Suggested stay: half-day town walk + one night at the market; Wucaitan needs half a day; reserve next day's drive for Kanas/Hemu.
    • Dress & sun: strong UV — hat, sunglasses, sunscreen; even summer days in short sleeves need a windproof jacket at night, huge day-night swing.
    • Local notes: order cold fish cooked-to-order; go deeper for authenticity; watch the weighing unit; book B&Bs early in peak season.
