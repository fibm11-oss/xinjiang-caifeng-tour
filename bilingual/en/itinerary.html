    How to choose

    What actually differs

    All three cover the same northern Xinjiang; what changes is how many days you can give it and how deeply you want to stop. The 17-day route adds both sides of the Tianshan and Turpan while still keeping several full rest days. The 11-day version drops the southern leg and some cities to keep the best lakes and grasslands. The 16-day version lingers longer between Ili and the Duku, with narration along the way.

    Principles shared by all three

    • Keep daily driving to roughly 2–7 hours, and follow a long day with a slow one
    • Schedule headline sights for early morning or late light, away from crowds and harsh midday sun
    • Include seasonal roads such as the Duku and Yizhao only during their open window, with a reroute in reserve
    • Choose overnight stops for the light, not for administrative boundaries
    Flights are illustrative. The flights and dates in these examples come from one real departure and change with each year's timetable. What matters is your arrival city and available days — we rebuild everything else around those.

    Changing season, adding southern Xinjiang, travelling with children or older relatives, or shifting departure times for photography: start from any of the three and we will adapt it.
