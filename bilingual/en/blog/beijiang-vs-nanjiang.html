    North vs south

    One mountain range, two very different Xinjiangs

    For most people the first question is not whether to come, but which side. Here is the difference, plainly.

    1. The difference starts with geography

    The Tianshan runs across the middle of Xinjiang, holding moisture to the north and leaving aridity to the south. The north receives Atlantic and Arctic air, which gives it grasslands, lakes, spruce and birch. The south, ringed by high mountains and desert, keeps its oases, ancient cities, yardang formations and a remarkably dense cultural texture.

    This is not about which is prettier. It is about what moves you: go north for the shape and light of the land itself, south for how people have lived in an extreme environment for two thousand years.

    2. The north: grassland, lakes, snow peaks

    • Highlights: Kanas, Hemu, Sayram Lake, the Ili valley, Nalati, the Duku Highway, Koktokay
    • Strength: dramatic seasonal change — flower seas, autumn gold and snow are three different journeys
    • Pace: long distances between sights, but the road itself is often part of the experience
    • Best: June–October; autumn colour peaks late September to early October

    The north suits first-timers, photographers, families, and anyone whose idea of Xinjiang is big landscape. With 8–11 days, one circuit of Ili or Altay is complete in itself; 15+ days is what a relaxed Grand Loop needs.

    A classic northern route: the 10-day Ili Valley circuit — Ürümqi, Sayram Lake, Yining, Zhaosu, Tekes, Nalati and the northern Duku. It is the most concentrated loop we know.

    3. The south: old cities, desert, culture

    • Highlights: Kashgar Old Town, the Kuqa Grand Canyon, the Pamir Plateau, the Taklamakan, Hotan
    • Strength: Uyghur and Tajik daily life, Silk Road remains, bazaars, crafts, extremely arid landforms
    • Pace: more time in cities — for wandering, conversations and food rather than ticking off sights
    • Best: March–May and September–November; desert edges are intensely hot in high summer

    The south often gives its best moments in unplanned half-hours: children chasing a ball through an old-town lane, a coppersmith's hammer, a pot of brick tea in a teahouse. If travel for you is about people and stories, the south will hold your attention longer.

    A classic southern route: a 9-day Silk Road journey through Ürümqi, Kuqa, Kashgar, Taxkorgan, the Pamirs and Hotan — linked by cities and oases, with a walkable neighbourhood almost every day.

    4. Seasons and trip length side by side

    • North: grasslands June–August, autumn gold late September; winter snow but far fewer routes
    • South: spring and autumn are most comfortable; avoid July–August heat; winter is quiet with short daylight
    • North: 8–11 days for one circuit, 15–17 for the Grand Loop
    • South: 8–10 days for the Silk Road; 12+ if adding the desert highway and the Pamirs

    5. Who each suits

    • First visit, families, photography, nature → north
    • Return visit, culture and bazaars, food, slow wandering → south
    • Only one week → do not be greedy; pick one concentrated route
    • 18+ days → combining both is possible, but expect long transfers

    6. Can you do both in one trip?

    Yes, but we rarely recommend it for a first visit. The connection between the two is long, and forcing it usually means a transfer every day and only the surface of each. Better to go deep on one side and return for the other — Xinjiang is worth two trips.

    If you do want both, start from 18–20 days and leave two empty recovery days in the middle.
