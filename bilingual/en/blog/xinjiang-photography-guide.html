    Photography

    Xinjiang Photography Calendar

    The same place in March and October is two different worlds. Here is the timed shot list.

    1. Why Xinjiang is so photogenic

    Inland air is dry and clean, so distant mountains hold their layers and a telephoto compresses them into an oil painting; snow peaks, grassland, desert and old towns share the frame with saturated colour; and the everyday life of the Uyghur, Kazakh and Tajik peoples is the best subject a lens can ask for. Clarity, contrast, humanity — Xinjiang gives you all three at once.

    2. Spring・Apricot blossom (late March–mid April)

    Where: Tuergen apricot valley (mid-to-late April) is the largest wild apricot forest left from the medieval period, pink-white blossom against snow peaks; Toksun (mid-to-late March) is Xinjiang's earliest spring; the Datong township apricot (April) is a pink-white village beneath the Pamir.

    • Shoot at dawn while thin mist lingers and light is soft; golden hour lights blossom and snow peaks
    • High vantage point to frame blossom and peaks together; front-light the flowers, back-light the silhouettes and fuzz
    • Bloom lasts barely ten days — check that year's forecast before you go

    3. Summer・Grassland bloom and lavender (June–July)

    Where: Huocheng lavender (mid-June–early July) purple sea against snow; Zhaosu rapeseed (July) a million mu of gold; Nalati and Kalajun grassland (June–August) three-dimensional pasture.

    • Dew and side-light at dawn; golden grassland at dusk; avoid the overhead midday sun
    • Kalajun's "human-body grassland" curves; Bayinbuluk's nine bends — wait for the last light of sunset and the river mirrors "nine golden rays"

    4. Autumn・Poplar and autumn colour (Sep–Nov, poplar late Oct–early Nov)

    Where: Kanas and Hemu (late Sep–early Oct) golden birch and log cabins, the textbook autumn; Luntai and Weili poplar forests (late Oct–early Nov) a golden great wall in the desert; Zepu golden poplar (late Oct) poplar reflected in water.

    • Morning mist at Hemu's Haden platform; golden-hour light on the groves
    • Birch and cabin smoke by the Hemu river; Kanas Fairy Bay mist; poplar reflections in water

    5. Winter・Snow and blue ice (Dec–Feb)

    Where: Hemu snow village, cabins and smoke with rime, pure storybook; Kanas blue ice, the frozen lake glowing deep blue; Sayram Lake ice bubbles and blue ice (Jan–Feb), the cracked surface and trapped bubbles are a photographer's holy site.

    • Blue hour (half an hour either side of sunrise) turns snow blue; crisp light right after a snowfall gives the strongest contrast
    • Hemu cabins and chimney smoke; Sayram's cracked ice and bubbles

    6. Gear and field rules

    • Anti-freeze: cold drains batteries fast, keep spares against your body; touch-screen gloves are essential
    • Wind and sand: a UV filter protects the glass; desert and highway stretches are gritty
    • Filters: polariser cuts glare on water and sky, graduated filter balances snow and sky brightness
    • Tripod: needed for mist, stars and the nine-bend sunset; mind it on icy ground
    • Respect: ask before photographing people, especially at mosques and in villages
