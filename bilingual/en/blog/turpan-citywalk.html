    City walk

    In the Furnace, follow the shade

    Turpan is not about how many stops you make. It is about what time you leave the hotel.

    The route at a glance

    Summer days can exceed 40°C, so this route deliberately leaves 12:00–16:00 empty. In other seasons, shorten the rest and add a stop.

    • 09:00–11:00 Old town & Gaochang Road
    • 11:00–12:00 Emin Minaret
    • 12:00–16:00 Rest / indoors
    • 16:00–18:00 Karez Folklore Park
    • Dusk: Grape Valley oasis
    • Dinner: night market

    Stop 1: Old town & Gaochang Road (09:00–11:00)

    Ochre walls, carved wooden doors and drying houses are Turpan's everyday palette. Before the heat builds, light is angled and shadows long — the best time for both walking and photographs.

    • See: old lanes, craft shops, raisin and nut stalls
    • Ask residents before photographing
    • Lanes are shaded and far more comfortable than the main road

    Stop 2: Emin Minaret (11:00–12:00)

    The Qing-era brick minaret carries geometric brickwork that becomes strikingly three-dimensional in hard light. You need not stay long, but it is the clearest lesson in the Islamic architecture of this oasis.

    Heat warning: return to the hotel or find an interior at midday. We write this gap into every Turpan day — there is no prize for pushing through.

    Stop 3: Karez Folklore Park (16:00–18:00)

    The karez are underground channels carrying Tianshan snowmelt into the oasis; without them there would be no Turpan grapes. You can walk a section of channel, noticeably cooler in summer — a stop that combines context and relief.

    Stop 4: Grape Valley (dusk)

    Grape trellises turn the whole valley into a green tunnel. Evening light comes through the leaves and the temperature finally drops. A Uyghur family visit with tea and a little muqam fits naturally here.

    • Grape season runs roughly July–September; varieties and sweetness shift by month
    • Home visits need booking in advance — mention dietary restrictions
    • Give Jiaohe Ancient City its own half day rather than squeezing it into this town route

    Stop 5: Night market (dinner)

    Hand-pulled noodles, grilled meat, baked buns and chilled yoghurt, with a breeze that only arrives after sunset. In Turpan, the day properly begins once the sun goes down.

    Practical information

    • Temperature: extreme in summer — sun protection, a hat and a water bottle are essential
    • Dress: modest clothing in religious sites; ask before photographs
    • Transport: sights are scattered, so a private vehicle saves a lot of time
    • Time: Beijing time on the clock, later in practice; dinner and markets come alive after dark
    Turpan, where the Silk Road meets the Furnace. It usually closes a Grand Northern Loop — about 190–200 km / 2.5–3 hrs back to Ürümqi, which lines up neatly with a departure flight.
