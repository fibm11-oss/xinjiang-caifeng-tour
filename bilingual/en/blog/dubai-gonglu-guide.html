    Duku Highway

    A road that is only open half the year

    From desert to snowline to canyon, 561 km cut straight through the Tianshan. But first, understand when you can actually drive it.

    1. Why this road is special

    The Duku Highway runs about 561 km from Dushanzi in the north to Kuqa in the south, crossing the heart of the Tianshan. It compresses a detour of over a thousand kilometres between northern and southern Xinjiang into a single day. Because altitude climbs from a few hundred metres to above 3,000, you pass gobi, grassland, snow peaks, lakes and red canyons within hours.

    "Four seasons in one day, different weather every ten miles" is not marketing copy — it is a road report. It can be snowing on the pass while the valley below is in full sun. That is also why the open season is so short.

    2. Opening season and controls

    The highway is generally open from early June to early October. The exact dates are announced each year according to snowmelt, rockfall and maintenance, and can shift by a week or two. It closes fully in winter, and night-time restrictions are common.

    • Check that year's official opening notice a week before departure — never rely on last year's dates
    • Heavy rain, rockfall or geological alerts can trigger temporary one-way or full closures
    • Some sections restrict vehicle types and speed, and night driving is prohibited; large vehicles and self-drivers should take particular care
    • If closed, the usual fallback links via motorway through Tekes, the river valleys or Kuitun
    For international travellers: self-driving requires a valid mainland Chinese licence or a temporary permit, with paperwork and insurance to match. Most visitors choose a private vehicle so a local driver handles conditions and controls — usually the more practical option here.

    3. Three sections and essential stops

    Northern: Dushanzi → Qiaoerma (approx. 130 km)

    Start at the ochre layers of the Dushanzi Grand Canyon and climb to the Hasilegen Pass. This is the most dramatic section and the most prone to sudden weather; the Qiaoerma area has a memorial and supply stops, and is a good place to break.

    Middle: Qiaoerma → Bayanbulak (approx. 140 km)

    Grassland and river valleys take over. The Nine Bends of Bayanbulak is the classic late-afternoon frame — worth an overnight rather than a drive-through if you have the time.

    Southern: Bayanbulak → Kuqa (approx. 290 km)

    Alpine meadow gives way to dry red canyon, with the Tianshan Grand Canyon and the Kizil Caves on this side. It is the longest section: give it a full day and add nothing else.

    4. Private car vs self-drive

    Self-driving offers the most freedom, but you own the licences, insurance, road-status checks, sudden closures and long hours of mountain driving. A private vehicle hands all of that to a local driver; you decide only where to stop and for how long — on a road this changeable, that is usually the more realistic choice.

    When we include the Duku, we normally plan one section rather than the whole road: for example Nalati over the northern section to Kuitun, paired with grassland and canyon days either side. That fits most people's energy and photography far better than forcing 561 km into one day.

    5. Before you go

    • Warm layers and sun protection together — passes can be near freezing
    • Motion-sickness tablets: many bends, fast altitude change
    • Plenty of water and snacks: long gaps between supply points
    • Cash and documents: mountain signal and mobile payment can be unreliable
    • Check fuel and tyre pressure before departure; winter equipment follows that year's rules

    6. Best light

    The northern section likes morning side light, which lifts the canyon strata. Keep Bayanbulak for the two hours before sunset. The southern red canyons are most saturated in low evening light. If you can only choose one, choose dusk at the Nine Bends.

    Do not treat the Duku as a single "attraction". It is a whole day in itself. A slow day on either side is far more comfortable than two consecutive long transfers.
