City walk

God's own reserve — walk it slowly to keep it

A Tuvan wooden-cabin village of cabins, golden birch and misty river valleys, like a village from a fairy tale.

Hemu, called by many "God's own reserve", is among the few remaining Tuvan villages in China, rated by Chinese National Geography as "China's most beautiful ancient village", and in 2025 selected among China's 50 best autumn spots. Wooden cabins, golden birch and a misty river valley make it a village from a fairy tale. For most, Hemu is the most worthwhile stop on a Kanas trip — no rushing sights, just slow walking and looking.

The route at a glance

  • Afternoon arrival: Hemu Bridge & river → birch woods → century-old house / Tuvan home
  • Dawn: Haden viewing platform (morning mist, sunrise)
  • Dusk: village wander / starry sky

Hemu's City Walk is simple: the village is small, the plank paths reach the main sights. But the real magic is the sense of time — dawn mist, afternoon herds returning, a night sky of stars; every moment worth pausing. We strongly suggest at least one overnight; otherwise mist and stars pass you by.

Hemu Bridge & river (afternoon arrival)

The Hemu Bridge is the village's soul landmark, built in 1918 by White Russians and rebuilt on site in 1970, all of raw timber, with gaps between the uneven planks — look down to see the rushing Hemu River, milky emerald from snowmelt. It is a recurring scene in the TV series "To the Wonder". The wooden bridge gates at both ends are the most photogenic; gate as foreground, snow mountain behind, the standard shot. Open May–Oct 08:00–22:00, off-season 10:00–18:00, no separate ticket.

Birch woods (dusk)

Past the bridge, ten minutes upstream along the west bank, the riverside birch belt — Hemu's goldenest spot in autumn. Mid-to-late September is peak, leaves most translucent yellow before they start dropping; a week later they fall. Sun slants into the woods, fallen gold leaves reflecting; dark clothes and a back-shot is a classic Hemu frame.

Century-old house & Tuvan home (dusk)

Hemu is a Tuvan (Mongol subgroup) village; all houses are plain little log cabins, notched logs with moss in the seams, scattered through the valley. The century-old house and lamasery can be visited; you may meet Tuvans playing the "suor" (reed flute) and throat-singing. If invited for a home visit, sit for a bowl of salty milk tea with fresh baked naan and listen to mountain stories — the warmest part of the walk.

Haden viewing platform (dawn, the highlight)

The Haden platform on the southwest hillside is the C-spot overlooking the whole village: cabins, birch, the Hemu River and distant snow peaks in one frame. The mist's golden window is about 40 minutes around sunrise — late September sunrise is about 7:30–8:00 Beijing time; fog rises from the valley floor first, then sunlight hits the mist, smoke and fog swirling — the most beautiful moment. Climb the plank path from the village, 20–30 minutes; dark and steep, a headlamp is essential; up there late September is 0–5°C with wind, full down jacket and gloves needed. Don't leave after shooting — about half an hour after sunrise the birch "light up", even better than the mist.

Village wander & starry sky (night)

After dark, the old village (around the bridge) is liveliest, the "Hemu CBD", with shops and easy transfers; away from light pollution the Milky Way is overhead. If energy allows, give half a day to Meili Peak — behind the village, horseback round-trip 3–4 hours, overlooking river-valley pasture; the gentle herder trail suits trekkers too. Summer's Hongbasi grassland is a sea of wildflowers, another Hemu.

Locals know: Hemu uses a combined ticket (includes shuttle, valid 48 hrs, multiple entries); peak May 1–Oct 15, off-season per official plan. Private cars banned; park at the transfer station and take the shuttle (40–60 min) into the village; village buses loop. Autumn gold window Sep 15–Oct 5 (fewer people before National Day; some areas close after Oct 5); Jul–Aug escape the heat with wildflowers, winter is a snow fairy tale. Where to stay? Old village is near the platform/bridge/dining, lively but cabins plain; new village is newer and quiet but needs transfer — photographers can stay near the platform. Mountain weather flips fast; before the platform, check the guesthouse owners' weather group — more accurate than any app.

Restaurants & cafés to try

Hemu is a Tuvan cabin village where you eat not just food but the mood of snow, birch and chimney smoke. Restaurants range from chain fast food to refined cabins; cafés are so many they seem to have moved the city's hipsters into the woods — push open any wooden door when tired and be surprised. Many choices; check before ordering.

Where locals eat

  • Ma's Xichu Restaurant: extremely high local reputation, tender lamb with no gaminess, honest portions — the village's公认 top shop for a proper Xinjiang meal.
  • Hemu Old Bridge Cold-Water Fish Restaurant: cold-water fish, delicate and boneless, with the cabin atmosphere a real ritual. For "river fresh" and a change, this is it.
  • Riverside Inn: one of the riverside cabins, with Kazakh grassland song-and-dance at meals, baked buns, huge portions. Eat while watching the snow river valley — Hemu's own relaxation.
  • Gesanghua Restaurant: on Sumuhe Road, rich breakfast, fresh baked buns — a proper convenient fill-up.

Cafés & drinks

  • Go Far Café: old village No. 285, cabin exquisite like a film set; push in on a winter night and it's like a Russian hunter's hut; snow falls outside mid-meal, atmosphere maxed.
  • Wild Growth Coffee · Concept Store: Weihala 187, sells far more than coffee — clothes, handmade jewellery, cultural postcards, all curated, not Yiwu wholesale, rare in Xinjiang.
  • Ge Coffee (Hemu): Donghala 81, quiet space to idle and watch the mountains; stable dark-roast beans, a cake makes an afternoon.
  • In the Mountains Coffee: on Jikepulin Road, the village's relatively friendly option; a cup on the way back after a long day, no burden.

Practical information

  • Getting there: fly to Kanas airport, ~2 hrs by car to the transfer station; or train to Beitun then carpool; self-drive park at Jiadenyu/transfer station for the shuttle. Village buses loop.
  • Best season: Sep 15–Oct 5 golden autumn (photographers' first choice); Jul–Aug wildflowers escape heat; winter snow for horse sleighs.
  • Suggested stay: at least one night (mist + stars); two nights more relaxed, add Meili Peak.
  • Dress & sun: mountain day-night swing up to 20°C, near 0°C at dawn; shell jacket/light down, non-slip shoes, headlamp, warm packs; plateau UV strong, sunglasses and sunscreen; keep spare batteries close in cold.
  • Local notes: hire正规 horse teams and agree terms first; no wild trails; ask before photographing Tuvans; village supplies come from outside, bring snacks and a thermos; weak signal, download offline maps; drones need filing.
