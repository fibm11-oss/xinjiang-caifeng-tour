City walk

The old town's slowness is tasted only by walking into its lanes

Kashgar is not a transit stop — a few lanes of the old town deserve a whole unhurried day.

Kashgar — "Kashgar" in Uyghur, the living oasis town on the Silk Road and the westernmost city in China. West of the centre, oases, snow mountains and foreign lanes intertwine; east of it, the edge of the Taklamakan Desert is not far. The city blends the breath of Central Asia, Persia and the Central Plains — earth-yellow rammed-earth homes, painted wooden doors, the charcoal scent of kebabs from street corners, and the never-ending rawap music in the old teahouses. Many treat Kashgar as a stopover to Pamir, but the old town's lanes alone are worth a full slow day.

The route at a glance

  • 09:30–10:30 Id Kah Mosque
  • 10:30–12:00 The century-old teahouse (morning tea)
  • 12:00–14:00 Osman Boyi Road・Craftsmen's Street
  • 14:00–16:00 Gaotai residential houses
  • 16:00–23:30 Hand Bazaar food street & night market
  • Evening (optional) Kunlun Tower night view

Kashgar runs about two hours behind Beijing time; breakfast peaks after 09:00 and dinner after 19:00, so don't rush the morning. Around 10:30 each day the old town holds an "opening ceremony" of song and dance at the east gate, recreating the Silk Road caravans entering the city.

Id Kah Mosque (09:30–10:30)

The largest mosque in Xinjiang, its yellow brick gatehouse and green glazed dome are solemn in the morning light. Friday's congregational prayer draws the biggest crowd — a sea of worshippers on the square, the pulse of Kashgar's faith. On ordinary early mornings it is quiet, good for watching elders in the sun and children running past. Remove shoes and dress modestly; outside prayer times visitors may enter the courtyard and colonnades.

The century-old teahouse (10:30–12:00)

On Osman Boyi Road, this teahouse dates to 1912 and is the oldest still standing in Kashgar, also a filming location for "The Kite Runner". The wooden upper gallery overlooks a thousand-year-old street market; local elders play the rawap and hand drum here daily. Order a pot of rose herbal tea with bahali cake, break naan into the tea — the traditional Uyghur way. Musicians often improvise in the afternoon; time slows in the tea smoke.

Tip: Thu–Sun gets crowded; arrive before 11:00 for quiet music. No outside food inside.

Osman Boyi Road・Craftsmen's Street (12:00–14:00)

The road in front of the teahouse is the old town's craft artery: spinning wheels in pottery workshops, the clang of copper shops, the coloured warp of Atlas silk looms. Most artisans inherit the trade for generations; if you watch entranced, the master may let you pull the clay. Deep in the lanes, the Gaotai residences are a maze of layered rammed-earth architecture; from the top platform you see the old town's rooftop waves.

Hand Bazaar food street (14:00–16:00 or 21:00–23:30 night market)

West of Id Kah Square, the Hand Bazaar is the old town's liveliest stomach. By day it is a market of dried fruit, kebabs and baked buns; at night the lanterns light up and the sizzle of charcoal and cumin fills the street. Fresh baked buns shatter crisp, red-willow kebabs balance fat and lean, and the gangzi rou — mutton slow-stewed in an enamel mug — with a piece of naan soaked in broth warms the gut. This is not a staged show; it is Kashgar's real everyday life.

Kunlun Tower (evening・optional)

A landmark tower near the old town; from the top you frame the whole old town with distant snow peaks. A tea lounge inside makes it a fine place to end the walk as the lanterns glow and the earth-yellow lanes turn gold-red. If energy allows, switch to the "Oil Painting Street" west of the old town to watch local painters and grab a corner coffee.

Locals know: Kashgar is two hours behind Beijing time — peaks are after 09:00 and 19:00. Always ask before photographing residents. For Pamir, get the border permit at the downtown service centre in Kashgar; don't wait until the county.

Restaurants & cafés to try

The old town is the centre of Southern Xinjiang's food universe. The picks below are local favourites with steady reputations and very low risk of a bad meal; cafés range from desert coffee to a literary rooftop. Hours may change — confirm before you go.

Where locals eat

  • Alizati Pilaf House (Tawuguzi Rd): long-grain rice braised in lamb fat with yellow carrot and big mutton chunks, rich yet not greasy; pair with juicy thin-skin buns. Go early.
  • Kaimeierding Blue Pigeon (Yumulake Xiehai'er Rd): clear stewed pigeon soup, bright and sweet, warming; roast pigeon crisp outside, firm inside — the standard breakfast with fresh baked buns.
  • Blue Ram Kebab (Tuman Rd): old-school red-willow kebabs, lamb on red-willow skewers over charcoal with a woody aroma; wrap in hot naan.
  • Grandpa Abū's Gangzi Rou (Qiasa Rd): three generations running it, mutton slow-stewed in enamel mugs, clear broth and tender meat; break naan to soak — a photogenic everyday scene.
  • Grandpa's Grandpa's Dad's Naan (old town west): baked to order, face-sized naan crisp outside, soft inside, full of wheat aroma — good travel food.

Cafés & drinks

  • Maze Coffee (Areya Rd・wooden-ware bazaar): carpets and vintage oil paintings, a local-style speciality, a slow immersion in old-town life.
  • Old Town Corner Coffee (Areya Rd): on a corner, the old town's daily life right outside the window; relaxed like a living room.
  • DOSTUM Old Street Punk Coffee (Osman Boyi Rd): Turkish-style coffee boiled in hot sand — no need to fly to Istanbul.

Practical information

  • Getting there: Kashgar airport and high-speed station reach the city directly; walk the old town, taxi outside; a border permit is needed for Tashkurgan.
  • Best season: Apr–Jun for apricot blossom and mild weather, Sep–Oct for crisp autumn; summer needs sun protection and dust care.
  • Suggested stay: one day City Walk (09:30–23:00); Pamir and the Karakoram Highway need one to two extra days.
  • Dress & sun: strong UV — sunscreen, sunglasses, hat; modest dress and shoe removal at mosque and teahouse.
  • Local notes: two-hour time lag, don't rush mornings; no pork or alcohol in halal restaurants; use Id Kah as your navigation centre to avoid getting lost.
