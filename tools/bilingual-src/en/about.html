Who we are

Local knowledge changes the route

Xinjiang is enormous — 1.66 million square kilometres, from the flower valleys of Ili to the snowline of the Pamirs. Getting the sequence right matters more than any single sight. We are a local team on these roads year after year: we know when the lavender blooms, when the Duku Highway opens, and which hour of light a particular lake deserves.

That is why we do not sell fixed packages. Every traveller gets an itinerary written from scratch — built around your dates, your pace and the people you travel with, with transfers, stays and stops settled before you land.

International travellers are very much within our scope. English communication, airport pick-ups, dietary requirements and prayer-time considerations are all confirmed with you before departure.

FAQ

Questions we hear most often

Q: Should I travel independently or join a tour?

Distances between sights often run into hundreds of kilometres, so fully independent travel can be demanding on logistics. Large group tours, on the other hand, tend to rush and include shopping stops. A private chartered trip sits between the two: someone handles the driving and local knowledge, while the pace stays entirely yours.

Q: Is Xinjiang safe to visit?

The main travel circuits are well established, with airports, visitor centres and towns all fully serviced. We brief you on local practicalities before departure and you are accompanied throughout by a local driver and guide. For border or restricted areas we help arrange the required permits in advance.

Q: When is the best season?

June to September brings grasslands, flowers and lakes at their best. Late September to early October is the golden autumn photographers come for in northern Xinjiang. The Duku Highway is usually open only from early June to early October, depending on snowmelt. Winter offers snow scenery and remarkably quiet sites.

Q: How many days do I need?

Allow 8–10 days for Ili, 8–10 for a Kanas loop, and 15 or more for a combined north–south itinerary. With less time, a four-day Duku Highway crossing or a Turpan highlights trip still works beautifully. We cut sights to fit your dates rather than cramming them in.
