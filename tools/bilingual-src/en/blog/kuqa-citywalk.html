City walk

Every painted gate is different — walk slowly to see it

Kuqa lacks Kashgar's clamour, yet has its own Qiuci romance, worth a full unhurried day.

Kuqa is the "Qiuci" of the history books — one of the thirty-six kingdoms of the Western Regions, the prototype of the "Kingdom of Women" in Journey to the West, and the hometown of the monk Kumarajiva. It was a Silk Road hub and the southern terminus of the Dushanzi–Kuqa Highway: descend from Northern Xinjiang and the road ends in this small town. Kuqa lacks Kashgar's clamour, yet has its own Qiuci romance — on Resitan Road every painted gate is different, the Kuqa naan is bigger than a steering wheel, the old street ends at the last prince's mansion, and the riverside bridge turns into a night market after dark. Wander slowly; the town is more walkable than you think.

The route at a glance

  • 09:30–10:30 Kuqa Grand Mosque
  • 10:30–12:30 Resitan Old Street・Qiuci Alley
  • 13:00–15:00 Kuqa Prince's Mansion
  • 15:30–17:00 Great Naan City
  • 20:00–23:00 Wuqia Night Market / Tuanjie New Bridge Night Market (dinner)

This is a Kuqa one-day City Walk designed for "slow old-town wandering": start at the Kuqa Grand Mosque, the second-largest in Xinjiang, stroll Resistan Street for painted gates and a plate of fried noodles, spend the afternoon in the Prince's Mansion reading Qiuci history, then watch face-sized naan pulled from the oven at the Great Naan City, and slip into the night market for kebabs and Uyghur dance after dark. The pace is relaxed — Kuqa's joy lives in the unhurried alleys.

Kuqa Grand Mosque (09:30–10:30)

Not far from Resitan Road, it is the second-largest mosque in Xinjiang after Id Kah in Kashgar, with nearly a century of history. The towering earthen-brick gatehouse and green glazed dome are solemn and sacred — a niche spot for cultural photography. In the morning light there are few visitors, good for quietly observing this Silk Road town's landmark of faith.

Resitan Old Street・Qiuci Alley (10:30–12:30)

The main artery of the old town: low, colourful houses line both sides, the arcades decorated in ethnic style, and no two doors share the same colour or totem — richer than many "internet-famous" streets. Qiuci Alley is the essence, with painted gates, flower tiles and old-tree shadows layered together, perfect for slow shooting. Old restaurants, cafés and guesthouses dot the street; Uyghur dance performances often start around 22:00.

Kuqa Prince's Mansion (13:00–15:00)

At the end of Linji Road in the old town, where Resitan Road begins, this was the last prince's residence — a palace blending Central Plains and Islamic styles, housing the Qiuci Museum that tells the story of twelve hereditary Kuqa princes. The last princess still lives in the mansion; with luck you may meet her. A few steps out brings you to the "Qiuci Ancient Ferry" (Tuanjie New Bridge) linking new and old towns, the Kuqa River flowing beneath; by dusk the bridgehead becomes a night market.

Great Naan City (15:30–17:00)

An old-town building at the crossing of Jianshe Road and Shengli Road; at the gate stands a super-sized naan taller than a person for photos. Inside, the first floor is a music square where local aunties sing and dance; the second floor sells naan, and a transparent workshop shows the whole process from kneading to baking in the naan oven — small naan scented with rose, large naan rich with onion and sesame. The old tea house is also upstairs; try naan with tea while carpets and curtains brim with exotic patterns.

Night market (20:00–23:00)

After dark, Kuqa's bridges and squares transform into smoky night markets: vegetables, fruit, kebab and naan stalls that make mouths water. Wuqia and Nanhu night markets are a bit more modern, but the food is equally authentic. Fresh baked buns are paper-thin and crisp with lamb-onion aroma; the bahdan (almond) ice cream is many people's Xinjiang favourite. Around 22:00, street-corner Uyghur dance appears — join the clapping crowd, most at ease.

Locals know: every painted gate on Resitan Road carries a different totem — walk slowly to spot the craft. The "Labaokou Bazaar" only runs on Sundays, a true market day of clothing, sundries and food. Having naan with tea in the Great Naan City's old tea house feels more local than any sight. The old town is not yet over-commercialised — few people, slow pace, a fine starting point for a Southern Xinjiang trip before heading to the Wensu Grand Canyon or the Dushanzi–Kuqa Highway. The night-market dance starts about 22:00, so don't leave too early.

Restaurants & cafés to try

Kuqa is a small Southern Xinjiang town very suited to "walk-and-eat": old-town kebabs, pilaf, baked buns and cafés are all there, richer than expected. The picks below are local favourites or repeatedly mentioned in travel notes, with low risk of a bad meal; hours may change — confirm before you go.

Where locals eat

  • Great Naan City (Jianshe Rd & Shengli Rd): the steering-wheel-sized Kuqa naan is the town's signature; small rose-scented naan, large onion-sesame naan. Watch the whole baking process in the transparent workshop; naan with tea in the old tea house, full of exotic atmosphere.
  • Wuzul Baheshan Restaurant (Resitan Rd): an old eatery on the old street, its Xinjiang ramen and fried noodles both good; regulars remind you "a bit of vinegar tastes better". After wandering, slip in for a hot bowl of noodle soup.
  • Zaipa'er Food City (Tian Shan West Rd, Qiuci Garden): European-style "palace" decor with local taste, with seats and private rooms, a place locals choose for everyday gatherings. Naan-wrapped kebab is safe; the qiu pian (pasta slices) is clean and flavourful.
  • Kuqa Bawangfu Fat Lamb Hotpot: super-sized fresh-cut beef and lamb, firm and fresh with no gaminess; sour-plum drink, popsicles and watermelon served at the table — a warm-up after a long day.
  • Patiguli Reheman Naan Shop (Resitan Rd): nobody resists a just-out-of-oven naan — savoury, crisp, big and filling. Near the crossing of Resitan and Pahata Bazaar roads; buy one and nibble while walking.

Cafés & drinks

  • Ka'erbowaye Ice Cream (Kuqa): for many, the best ice cream in Xinjiang, the bahdan (almond) flavour dense and fragrant; a scoop on the old street revives you instantly. Long summer queues are normal.
  • Wangfu Baked Bun Shop (by the Prince's Mansion exit): thin-skin baked buns right outside the mansion, crust shattering crisp, lamb-onion filling juicy; a cup of Xinjiang yoghurt is the classic pairing.

Practical information

  • Getting there: Qiuci airport and railway station reach the city directly; Kuqa is the southern terminus of the Dushanzi–Kuqa Highway — walk the old town, taxi outside.
  • Best season: May–Oct for pleasant weather; summer is hot, winter cold, but old-town wandering stays comfortable.
  • Suggested stay: one day City Walk (with night market) is just right; nearby Kizil Caves, Tianshan Mysterious Grand Canyon and Subashi Temple need separate days.
  • Dress & sun: strong UV, dry climate — sunscreen, sunglasses, hat; modest dress at mosque and mansion.
  • Local notes: Sunday's Labaokou Bazaar is liveliest; the old town is not over-commercialised and uncrowded for photos; ask before photographing residents; buy from proper shops.
